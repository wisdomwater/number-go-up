**The number is improving. So why does everything feel like it's getting worse?**

Every organization measures performance. It has to. But the moment a number becomes important, people begin responding to it. Engineers optimize for productivity metrics. Employees learn which numbers keep them out of trouble. Managers trust dashboards that necessarily leave out much of the work happening underneath them.

Nobody has to be foolish or dishonest for the system to become absurd. Everyone can make perfectly rational decisions—and still end up somewhere nobody intended to go.

*Number Go Up* is a funny, humane exploration of what happens when the measures we create to understand reality begin replacing it.

Drawing on decades inside the software industry as well as his own struggles with self-measurement, Michael Collins examines metrics from both sides of the dashboard: the people who need the map and the people who live in the territory.

This isn't an argument against data. It's an argument for knowing what data can—and cannot—tell us.

**Measure what matters. Just remember that what matters will always be more than what we can measure.**
